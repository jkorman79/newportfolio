Brewing Better Returns: A Data-Driven Approach to a New Starbucks Marketing Campaign

As the holiday season comes to a close, I find myself reflecting on the unique charm of Starbucks' timely festive marketing campaigns. My passion for data-driven marketing solutions led me to research and explore how Starbucks could improve its advertising approach moving forward. This project was not only a chance to hone my skills using real-world data, but also a timely opportunity to set Starbucks up for a successful new year.

I've always loved the idea of using data to solve marketing questions, especially when it comes to crafting effective advertising strategies. Starbucks' iconic holiday campaigns inspired me to see how data could enhance their impact and drive better customer engagement.

What You'll Learn

By reading this article, you'll discover key insights into Starbucks' customer behavior, identify high-value segments, and understand how targeted marketing could enhance their business strategy. I'll share findings like the purchasing habits of Starbucks' customers, and offer a recommendation for their future campaigns.

Key Takeaways

  • Most Starbucks purchases occur in winter, and primarily at night.
  • Many high-spending customers have not made any purchases in the last few months.
  • Suburban and uptown stores are more popular than downtown locations (i.e. they have a higher number of total sales).

About This Data

I analyzed a dataset from Kaggle with over 85,000 rows, which captures information such as order date, season, purchase items, customer demographics, and much more. This comprehensive data was crucial in identifying patterns and informing strategic decisions.

Analysis

Starting with data cleaning and transformation, I used segmentation and RFM analysis to determine the best marketing campaigns to try out that are best suited for Starbucks' customers. The results were both intriguing and unexpected. For instance, most purchases happened at night, contrary to the understandable expectation that most people would purchase coffee in the morning. This led to insights into customer preferences and timing, shaping the strategic recommendations.

Visuals and Insights

Cluster Dendrogram

Used to determine the number of customer segments to use for analysis. Based on this result, I decided to use three customer segments for K-Means Cluster analysis, which I determined by drawing a straight line down from the number 10 at the top of the dendrogram.

Segmentation Results

A table that shows behavioral information about all three segments found. This table showcases purchase behaviors and loyalty membership among all three clusters. In the end, I decided to use the first segment (highlighted), since those customers tend to spend the most per transaction on average. This segment proved vital in understanding high-value customers.

Purchases by Time of Day

This bar chart illustrates seasonal purchasing trends, confirming that most purchases occur at night, with mornings closely behind.

Purchases by Season

This bar chart revealed that winter and spring are the peak seasons for Starbucks, emphasizing the importance of targeting the ads at this time period.

Store Location Sales

Surprisingly, suburban and uptown stores outperformed downtown, indicating where marketing efforts could be concentrated.

Main Takeaways

Starbucks' strategy should focus on re-engaging high-spending customers who haven't returned recently. Targeted marketing campaigns during the winter nights could capitalize on peak purchasing times. Emphasizing suburban and uptown areas in advertising could further boost sales.

Conclusion and Personal Reflections

This project taught me the power of data in revealing unexpected customer behaviors, such as the prominence of nighttime purchases. By overcoming my initial assumptions and diving deep into data analysis, I developed a clearer picture of strategic opportunities. My recommendation for Starbucks is to craft targeted advertisements focusing on high-value segments during key seasons, especially winter.
